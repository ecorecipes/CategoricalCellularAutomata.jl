Spaces and gluing

Research extension: geometries beyond groups, as neighbourhood spaces and the maps between them, and landscapes glued from patches.

Geometries

Spaces beyond groups: the cells of any CellularAutomataCore space with their neighbour lists, and the maps between spaces that preserve neighbours, along which configurations pull back.

CategoricalCellularAutomata.NeighbourhoodSpace — Type
NeighbourhoodSpace(neighbours; pinned=Dict{Int,Any}())

A finite space: cells 1:n (the free cells, in the order of a CellularAutomataCore state, first axis fastest) followed by pinned cells, each with its neighbours as an ordered list of cells. A pinned cell holds a fixed value (pinned[cell]), the state beyond a Constant edge; its neighbours are itself. Built with neighbourhood_space; a configuration is a vector over the free cells.

As a mathematical object, a space whose cells all have k neighbour slots is a coalgebra of X ↦ X^k, and a space with lists of varying length (a graph) a coalgebra of the list functor; SpaceMorphisms are the maps between them.

source
CategoricalCellularAutomata.neighbourhood_space — Function
neighbourhood_space(space, neighbourhood=nothing) -> NeighbourhoodSpace
neighbourhood_space(τ::CellularAutomaton) -> NeighbourhoodSpace

The cells of a CellularAutomataCore space (a lattice of any shape and edges, or a GraphSpace) with the neighbours neighbourhood gives them (the space's default if nothing), in the order the core passes them to a rule. Beyond a Periodic edge a cell reads the opposite side, beyond a Reflecting edge its mirror image, and beyond a Constant edge a pinned cell holding the constant (one pinned cell for each constant; where several Constant axes are crossed at once, the highest-numbered one's value, as in the core). On a GraphSpace the neighbourhood must be GraphNeighbours() (or nothing).

The second form gives the Cayley space of a cellular automaton over a torus or cyclic group: each cell's neighbours are the cells its memory set reads, the identity left out.

CategoricalCellularAutomata.cayley_space — Function
cayley_space(G, generators) -> NeighbourhoodSpace

The Cayley space of a cyclic or torus group G marked with generators (group elements, as tuples for a torus group or integers for a cyclic one): cell g reads g + s for each generator s, in order. A square torus with von Neumann or Moore neighbours and a hexagonal torus are Cayley spaces of the same group Z_n × Z_m with different generators.

CategoricalCellularAutomata.apply_local — Function
apply_local(f, S::NeighbourhoodSpace, x) -> Vector
apply_local(rule, S, x; p=nothing) -> Vector

One synchronous step on the space S of the local rule f(cell, neighbours) (neighbours a tuple in the order of each cell's list), or of a deterministic CellularAutomataCore rule (called with parameters p, at time 0), from the configuration x over the free cells. Pinned cells keep their values.

apply_local(f, g::SlotGraph, x) -> Vector

One synchronous step on the slot graph g of the local rule f(cell, neighbours) from the configuration x over all cells of g: each interior cell reads its neighbours in slot order, and halo cells keep their values. On a patch of a glued landscape this is the landscape's step restricted to the patch's interior, given the halo's values from the landscape.

CategoricalCellularAutomata.SpaceMorphism — Type
SpaceMorphism(dom, codom, map, slots=nothing)

A map of spaces φ: dom → codom (NeighbourhoodSpaces): map[y] is the cell of codom that free cell y of dom goes to, and slots[y] a permutation of y's neighbour slots (the identity if slots is nothing). It preserves neighbours when the i-th neighbour of y goes to the slots[y][i]-th neighbour of φ(y) (a pinned neighbour going to a pinned cell with the same value): see is_morphism.

Pulling configurations back along such a map (pullback) commutes with every local rule invariant under the slot permutations: running the rule on codom and pulling back gives the same as pulling back and running it on dom. With identity permutations that is every rule (a morphism of coalgebras); reflection_fold needs rules symmetric under reflection.

source
CategoricalCellularAutomata.is_morphism — Function
is_morphism(f::SpaceMorphism) -> Bool

Whether f preserves neighbours: for every free cell y of the domain and slot i, the image of y's i-th neighbour is the slots[y][i]-th neighbour of the image of y, and every slot permutation is a permutation of y's slots of the right length.

CategoricalCellularAutomata.covering — Function
covering(big, small, neighbourhood=nothing) -> SpaceMorphism

The covering of a periodic lattice small by a periodic lattice big whose sides are multiples of small's: cell I goes to mod1(I, size(small)). It preserves neighbours slot by slot, so a cellular automaton on small is the one on big restricted to periodic configurations.

source
CategoricalCellularAutomata.reflection_fold — Function
reflection_fold(space, neighbourhood=nothing) -> SpaceMorphism

A lattice with Reflecting edges (and periodic ones) as a quotient of a torus: the domain is the torus with each reflecting side doubled, and a cell y goes to y on the first half of such an axis and to its mirror image 2n + 1 - y on the second. On the mirrored half neighbours lie in the opposite direction, so the slots are permuted (an offset o to the offset with that axis's component negated); the neighbourhood must contain those offsets. Only rules symmetric under these reflections commute with pulling back along it.

source
AlgebraicInterfaces.compose — Method
compose(f::SpaceMorphism, g::SpaceMorphism) -> SpaceMorphism
id(S::NeighbourhoodSpace) -> SpaceMorphism

Neighbourhood spaces and the maps between them form a category, an instance of Catlab's ThCategory: compose(f, g) is f followed by g (cells go through both maps and slot permutations compose), raising DomainMismatchError unless the codomain of f is the domain of g; id(S) is the identity of S. Composites of maps that preserve neighbours preserve them, and pulling back along compose(f, g) is pulling back along g, then f.

Gluing spaces

Spaces as C-sets of cells and neighbour slots, patches with halos, and landscapes glued from patches as colimits along undirected wiring diagrams.

SchSlotGraph

The schema of SlotGraph: objects Cell and Slot, morphisms reader, read: Slot → Cell, and an attribute index: Slot → Index.

source
CategoricalCellularAutomata.SlotGraph — Type
SlotGraph{T}()

A space as a C-set (schema SchSlotGraph): parts Cell and Slot, maps reader, read: Slot → Cell and an attribute index: Slot → T (an Int, the slot's position in the reader's neighbour list). Slot s says that cell reader(s) reads cell read(s) as its index(s)-th neighbour. Cells without slots are halo cells: read, but not updated, by the space. Build one with slot_graph or lattice_patch, or by hand with add_parts!; NeighbourhoodSpace turns a closed one back into a space. Maps of slot graphs are Catlab ACSetTransformations, and slot graphs have all colimits (pushouts glue them).

source
CategoricalCellularAutomata.OpenSlotGraph — Type
OpenSlotGraph{T}(g::SlotGraph{T}, legs::FinFunction...)

An open slot graph: a SlotGraph with legs, functions from finite sets of cells (the feet) into it; Catlab's structured (multi)cospans with discrete feet (Baez and Courser, 2020). Composites are taken with glue (or Catlab's oapply, compose and otimes); the legs are Catlab's legs, the slot graph its apex.

CategoricalCellularAutomata.NeighbourhoodSpace — Method
NeighbourhoodSpace(g::SlotGraph; boundary=nothing)

The space of a slot graph: its interior cells (those with slots, in order) are the free cells, each reading its neighbours in slot order. Every cell's slots must be numbered 1:k once each (two glued patches that both update a cell give it its slots twice). Halo cells that are read become one cell pinned to boundary (as beyond a Constant edge); with boundary = nothing there must be none. Halo cells nobody reads are dropped.

source
CategoricalCellularAutomata.lattice_patch — Function
lattice_patch(space::Lattice, neighbourhood=nothing) -> OpenSlotGraph{Int}

The cells of the lattice space (square or hexagonal; its edges are ignored) as an open slot graph: each cell reads neighbourhood (the space's default if nothing), and the cells beyond the edges that it reads are halo cells (see patch_coordinates). The legs are the faces, two for each axis d, in the order axis 1 low, axis 1 high, axis 2 low, …: the low face is the strip of positions 1 - r:r on axis d (the halo and the first r layers, r the neighbourhood's reach along d) across the padded box, the high face n - r + 1:n + r, both in the padded box's order. Gluing the high face of one patch to the low face of another (with glue) sets them side by side along axis d: each one's halo becomes the other's edge cells.

source
CategoricalCellularAutomata.patch_coordinates — Function
patch_coordinates(space::Lattice, neighbourhood=nothing) -> Vector{CartesianIndex}

The position of each cell of lattice_patch(space, neighbourhood): the cells of the lattice in order (first axis fastest), then the halo, the positions beyond the edges within the neighbourhood's reach on each axis (as 0, -1, … or n + 1, …).

CategoricalCellularAutomata.glue — Function
glue(diagram, patches; algorithm=:union_find)
    -> (landscape::OpenSlotGraph, inclusions::Vector{Vector{Int}})

Glue open slot graphs (lattice_patches, or any OpenSlotGraph) along the undirected wiring diagram diagram (from Catlab's @relation): each box is a patch, named in patches (a Dict or NamedTuple from box names to patches; a patch may fill several boxes), its ports its legs in order, and legs on the same junction are identified cell by cell (their feet must have the same size). The landscape is the colimit (a pushout for two patches, a coequaliser for a patch glued to itself), with legs for the diagram's outer junctions so it can be glued again; inclusions[b] sends each cell of box b's patch to its cell in the landscape. Close it with NeighbourhoodSpace(apex(landscape); boundary).

The feet are discrete (cells, no slots), so the colimit identifies cells only and its slots are the disjoint union of the patches' slots: the default algorithm = :union_find computes it so, in time linear in the size of the patches. algorithm = :catlab takes it with Catlab's oapply for structured cospans instead: the same landscape up to the order of its cells, but slow beyond a few hundred cells.
